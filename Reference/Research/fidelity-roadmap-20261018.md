# Press78 fidelity requirements and current evidence

The active objective is a premium, synth-focused PSS-780 model, validated against public circuit documentation and real recordings. A claim that it is the market's most accurate model remains unproven. This ledger preserves that full objective while recording concrete progress; neither passing software contracts nor a better score on one reused video completes it.

## Current evidence-backed work

- **Operator waveforms:** a newly acquired PSS-680 carrier-only table experiment supports the current four waveform identities, including signed squared sine. It does not identify numerical ROM precision or DAC encoding. See [recording audit](recording-holdout-audit-20261018.md).
- **D1 envelope endpoint:** the zero-height D1 stage no longer takes an unwanted decrement before D2. Controlled sibling behavior and explicit model endpoint tests support this bounded correction; factory changes are small. See [FM audit](fm-fidelity-audit-20261018.md).
- **Modulation:** reused PSS-780 pitch contours support about 5.24 Hz triangle modulation, with approximately 12.5 cents at PMS3 and 100 at PMS6. The frozen candidate improves pitch tracking on separately acquired PSS-580 Saw and Human Voice passages, with patch-identity and per-passage phase qualifications. Unobserved sensitivity levels and exact reset/delay rules remain hypotheses. See the [pitch audit](lfo-pitch-audit-20261018.md), [preregistered candidate](lfo-candidate-protocol-20261018.json) and [independent recording evaluation](recording-holdout-audit-20261018.md).
- **Analog integration:** the documented PSS-480 surrogate has a validated4x research component. End-to-end interpolation/circuit/decimation reduces its48kHz10kHz numerical magnitude error from1.43dB to0.076dB against the same continuous network. A tested80-host-frame matched-delay integration was rejected as the instrument default after it worsened75/100 PSS-780 comparisons. The shipping path retains its prior32-tick latency. Numerical accuracy to the sibling circuit does not establish exact-device agreement. See [schematic audit](output-schematic-audit-20261018.md) and `Tests/results/analog-oversampled.json`.
- **No added noise:** white and circuit-noise generation remain absent. Legacy noise properties are hidden/inert. Fidelity research does not override the user's explicit removal requirement.
- **Two-stage release evidence:** the exact-device tour's final pure-carrier chord has a fast release followed by a much slower tonal tail near 18–20 dB below its held level. Fixed-frequency/window analysis is independently reproduced. A fixed Reverb-cap experiment improves this extended tail, but global timing/cap candidates retain broad regressions and unresolved endpoint/allocation behavior. The shipping engine remains b11. See the [measured tail](exact-sine-release-20261018.md), [fixed timing experiments](envelope-law-benchmark-20261018.md) and [Reverb experiment](reverb-envelope-benchmark-20261018.md).
- **Capture separation:** a simultaneously held sine weakens a common-gain-only explanation of the released sine's two stages. Final partials disappear at different times in both AAC and Opus, excluding an Opus-transcode-specific or final-PCM16-only cause. Shared upstream processing remains unresolved; no new cutoff is adopted. See the [forensic controls](envelope-capture-forensics-20261018.md) and [codec comparison](codec-tail-audit-20261018.md).
- **Benchmark timing:** the first 18 selected instant-attack audible transitions are 22–63 ms later than the frozen MIDI mapping, but separate metadata-selected Jazz Guitar transitions are 55.7–68.1 ms earlier. The [global mapping validation](global-event-mapping-20261019.md) rejects all three fixed corrections; neither the old map nor a replacement is established as actual delivered MIDI timing. The [original audit](midi-alignment-audit-20261018.md) and its failures remain preserved.
- **Timing and model selection:** a fixed [four-map × three-model experiment](timing-sensitivity-20261019.md) reproduces all three prior unchanged-map renders byte-for-byte and retains all 100 scores for every scenario. Human Voice 1 favors the slower release/Reverb candidates under the old map and b11 under every corrected scenario. There are 22–26 per-patch RR×3/b11 band-ranking reversals. The full 100 mean band ordering remains b11, RR×3, then RR×3+Reverb, while STFT-convergence ordering reverses. No map or envelope replacement is selected by these reused-data scores.
- **Pitch and repeated-note behavior:** [fixed cross-pitch fits](rks0-release-slopes-20261019.md) find steeper decay for MIDI 87 than 79 at raw RKS0 in mid/pair measurements; moving stereo channels, prior-note history and harmonic overlap qualify the inference. The [manufacturer/editor evidence](opu-rks-evidence-20261019.md) establishes encoding but not the complete rate law. A [retrigger dip](retrigger-allocation-audit-20261019.md) exceeds what a restricted instantaneous phase-only step predicts, without identifying a physical allocator or delivered off/on gap.
- **Endpoint separation:** [fixed before/after windows](dac-endpoint-evidence-20261019.md) show MIDI 87 dropping 35–38 dB while other partials fall only 2–3 dB in AAC and Opus. One common scalar gate cannot be the sole explanation. Per-voice retirement, postmix quantization and shared upstream processing remain candidates; no DAC word length or hardware threshold is inferred.
- **Static pitch intervals:** the [captured pitch audit](pitch-grid-audit-20261019.md) retains the historical windows and finds repeated compression of about 0.48 cents in the simultaneous MIDI 67/75 interval in AAC and Opus. Controls expose single-channel Chorus bias. Using the 12 Hz phase estimator in the two original windows, the corresponding b11 mid control remains within 0.0005 cents. This constrains a common-mistuning-only explanation of the captured interval; it does not identify a hardware frequency grid or exclude shared-master processing.
- **Digital interface:** a [primary disassembly review](opu-digital-primary-leads-20261019.md) independently verifies the posted poll/write software pattern; its mapping to the FM block remains provisional. Conditional standard-65C02 cycle counts do not establish the Yamaha CPU clock, FM cadence, register meanings or arithmetic precision. No artificial scheduling quirk is added from those counts.
- **Additional source leads:** a [firsthand early-note-off account](early-noteoff-leads-20261019.md) motivates equal-known-RR tests. Ordinary asymmetric release already provides a competing explanation, so no special freeze rule is added. The [separate PSS-680 hardware/editor tutorial](soden-pss680-source-audit-20261019.md) supplies readable settings and transport constraints, but no defensible new numerical law: its linked editor uses single-voice SysEx dumps, Program Changes and a one-second auto-put timer.

## Market scope

[Plogue's own PortaFM page](https://www.plogue.com/products/chipsynth-portafm.html) identifies its current supported engines as OPLL/YM2413, OPL2/YM3812 and OPK2/YM7129. [discoDSP's own OPL page](https://www.discodsp.com/en/opl/) identifies an OPL2/partial-OPL3 instrument. These are relevant portable-FM comparators, but neither page establishes an exact YM3420/PSS-780 model. This bounded search is not proof that no other competitor exists and does not justify a superlative accuracy claim.

Completion requires measured agreement in the still-open behaviors above, plus complete instrument validation. Each further change should preserve raw source/capture provenance, separate calibration from evaluation, retain regressions and explain what is still unidentified.

The [optional controlled capture protocol](hardware-capture-protocol-20261018.md) prepares the next envelope investigation: independently authored pure-carrier voices, fixed gates, TL/rate sweeps and four matched Reverb/Sustain conditions. Its six MIDI files and 60 cases pass file-format, checksum and schedule validation. Generation performs no playback; deliberate use writes user bank 5 and requires the documented backup. No hardware measurements have yet been made with this kit.
